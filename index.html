Ossia: fully renewable, a hypothetical. The same moment re-scored as if the grid ran on wind, sun and water alone. Gas, coal, nuclear, biomass, imports and other are set to zero, and their share goes to wind, solar and hydro in proportion to what each really generated. NESO counts all three at 0 g CO₂/kWh, so the ossia plays at 0 g, in D Lydian, luminoso. Switch it on and off as it plays to hear the difference: louder sounds better to most ears, so the ossia is matched to the real grid's loudness first.

No wind, sun or water at all this half-hour, so there is nothing to scale up: the ossia gives the whole grid to wind, the renewable that can run at any hour.

How it sounds

Each source of power has its own staff and its own voice. The bigger its share of Britain’s electricity, the louder it plays: amplitude follows the square root of the share, and the dynamic under its staff, from 𝆏𝆏𝆏ppp to 𝆑𝆑ff, says how big a share that is. A source that isn’t generating rests.

The music is always in D at 96 beats a minute, in eight-bar phrases that rise to a pause half-way and then come home. Carbon intensity chooses the mode, which the score writes as its key signature: a clean grid plays in the bright modes with an open tone, a dirty one turns minor and closes in, and its drone starts to beat.

The cleaner the grid, the brighter the key: D Lydian below 60 g CO₂/kWh, D major from 60 g, D Mixolydian from 110 g, D Dorian from 150 g, D minor from 190 g and D Phrygian from 240 g.

The page reads left to right through the last two hours, a bar for each half-hour. The bars before the red cursor are half-hours already past, engraved from their own figures and marked with their time; the last bar is the live half-hour, written note by note as you hear it, and the page moves on a bar when NESO publishes the next one. A replayed day is written an hour a bar and turns its pages as it plays.

      About the data

      Figures come from the Carbon Intensity API, published by the National Energy System Operator (NESO, formerly National Grid ESO) under CC BY 4.0. They cover Great Britain in half-hour settlement periods; NESO marks the generation mix as beta. Intensity is NESO’s measured figure where it has published one and its forecast otherwise, and the page says which.

      Figures are for the last completed half-hour. Regional figures, chosen under “Composed by the grid of”, are NESO’s modelled estimates rather than measurements, and the page labels them that way.

      The page asks for the latest half-hour a few minutes after each one is due (every five minutes if it is late) and keeps the week’s history for 25 minutes. Pressing a record costs two requests for its day. If NESO can’t be reached the page says so, and plays a recording of a real day instead, labelled with its date.
