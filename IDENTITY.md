**Medium:** an engraved orchestral score, the full-score page of a printed edition (Bärenreiter, Henle), read in real time like a score-following cursor; a pressed day becomes the first page of its edition, as a record sleeve.
**Type:** one family, IBM Plex, served from the page itself: Plex Sans to read (the notes, the controls, tempo and *mesto* on the score), Plex Mono for data and records (times over the bars, bar numbers, figures, liner notes), Plex Sans Condensed for signs (the wordmark, headings, instrument names, the sleeve's title); Noto Music only for notation (clefs, rests, accidentals, real dynamics glyphs), because notation must come from a music font and Plex has none.
**Palette:** ink `#1a1714` on score paper `#f4efe6`; one red, `#c23b22`, the conductor's pencil, for the cursor, focus and anything live (`#a8301b` where it is small text).
**Motion rule:** nothing moves except the music being written: the half-hours already past stand engraved, the live bar is inked note by note as it sounds with the red cursor following, and the page moves on a bar only when NESO publishes the next half-hour (a replayed day turns its pages every four bars). Reduced motion writes whole bars at once and drops the cursor.
**Refused:** colour-coded lanes, generative squiggles, glows and gradients, decorative glyphs that aren't notation, icons, pills and badges, a tagline, a hero headline.
